# Energy-Friendly Routines

Most routines are written for steady energy: wake up at 6, exercise, work
eight hours, repeat. ADHD energy is rarely steady. It spikes, crashes, and
shifts without warning.

An energy-friendly routine flexes with your energy instead of breaking when
your energy dips.

## Map your energy

For a few days, jot down your energy level at morning, midday, and evening.
Use a simple scale: low, medium, high. You can use the
[Energy Map worksheet](../worksheets/energy-map.html).

You will start to see patterns. Maybe your brain is sharpest after lunch.
Maybe evenings are for lying down. That is useful information, not a problem
to fix.

## Match tasks to energy

Once you know your patterns, match tasks to the energy you actually have:

| Energy level | Good for | Not good for |
|---|---|---|
| High | Hard decisions, starting new projects, paperwork | Nothing — go for it |
| Medium | Email, dishes, tidying, short errands | Big new projects |
| Low | Rest, water, one tiny task, staring out the window | Anything requiring focus |

When your energy is low, the right move is often rest — not pushing through.

## Build a flexible routine

Instead of a fixed schedule, try a menu:

**Morning (any time after you wake up):**
- Drink water
- Open the curtains
- Pick one tiny task (optional)

**When energy is medium:**
- One household task (10 minutes max)
- One message or email
- A short walk

**When energy is low:**
- Rest without guilt
- One tiny task if you want
- Nothing at all

The menu lets you choose what fits, instead of following a script that was
written for a different brain.

## Routines are scaffolds, not rules

A routine is there to hold you up, not to punish you when you miss it. Skip
days. Change it. Throw it away and make a new one. The routine serves you —
you do not serve the routine.
