Tiny Tasks

Big tasks feel vague, heavy, and full of hidden steps. Tiny tasks make the first step so small it feels ridiculous to skip.

What a tiny task is

A tiny task is a single action that takes about two minutes or less, and needs no decisions to start.

  • Put one dish in the dishwasher — not "clean the kitchen"
  • Open the document and write one sentence — not "write the report"
  • Put on your walking shoes — not "go for a walk"
  • Take one pill and drink a glass of water
  • Write down three things you need to do today

If you still feel resistance, make it smaller. "Open the document" becomes "touch the mouse." There is no such thing as too small.

The two-minute rule, gently

If something takes less than two minutes and you have the energy for it, do it now instead of writing it down. If you do not have the energy, that is fine — write it down and come back when you do.

This is a tool, not a rule. You are allowed to ignore it.

How to break a task down

Pick one thing you have been avoiding. Then ask, again and again, "What is the very first physical step?"

Example: "Do laundry"

  1. Walk to the laundry basket
  2. Pick up three clothes
  3. Put them in the washer
  4. Add soap
  5. Press start

You do not have to finish the whole list. You only have to do step 1.

When tiny tasks are not enough

Some days, even a tiny task feels like too much. On those days, the task is rest. Lie down. Drink water. Breathe. Coming back later is always an option.
